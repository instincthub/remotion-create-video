## VIDEO SCRIPT

**[HOOK — 0:00–0:10]**

So… 80% of enterprise Artificial Intelligence projects fail. Eighty percent. Meanwhile, your smart speaker at home is nailing your music requests like a champ. What's going on? Why is Artificial Intelligence crushing it in your living room but stumbling in the boardroom?

**[CONTEXT — 0:10–0:40]**

Here's the thing. We interact with incredible Artificial Intelligence every single day. Google answers our questions. Amazon knows what we want before we do. Translation tools let us order coffee in Tokyo without learning a single word of Japanese. It feels like Artificial Intelligence is everywhere, and it is. But here's the twist most people miss: the Artificial Intelligence that runs your favorite apps and the Artificial Intelligence that runs actual businesses? Totally different methodology.

**[MAIN POINT 1: Consumer AI — The Trivia Champion — 0:40–1:05]**

Think of consumer Artificial Intelligence like a trivia night champion. You ask, "Who was Prime Minister during the Suez Crisis?" and it scans millions of web pages, finds "Anthony Eden" repeated thousands of times, and serves you the answer. Easy. Massive data. Tons of redundancy. Millions of users giving feedback to fine-tune the results.

**[MAIN POINT 2: Enterprise AI — The Courtroom Drama — 1:05–1:30]**

Now imagine asking an Artificial Intelligence inside an insurance company: "Should we approve this claim based on these 47 contradictory documents?" That's not trivia night. That's a courtroom drama with missing evidence. Enterprise Artificial Intelligence deals with way less data, far more complexity, and questions where the difference between right and almost-right actually matters.

**[MAIN POINT 3: The Stakes Are Higher — 1:30–1:55]**

And here's the kicker: the consequences of getting it wrong are completely different. If Alexa plays the wrong song, you laugh about it at dinner. If a police Artificial Intelligence flags the wrong person, or a medical Artificial Intelligence suggests the wrong diagnosis, that's not funny. That's someone's life. Enterprise Artificial Intelligence doesn't get the luxury of being "mostly right."

**[MAIN POINT 4: Why Projects Fail — 1:55–2:25]**

So why do companies keep stumbling? Because they see Artificial Intelligence work brilliantly in a proof-of-concept demo and assume it'll scale. But enterprise environments are messy. Data is contradictory. Experts are few. Feedback loops are tiny. And the jump from "cool demo" to "actual business value" is where most projects quietly die. Analysts estimate only 20 to 40 percent of enterprise Artificial Intelligence initiatives actually deliver real results.

**[PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY — 2:25–2:45]**

So what's the move? If you're bringing Artificial Intelligence into your organization, stop comparing yourself to Google. Recognize that your data is smaller, your questions are harder, and your margin for error is thinner. Design for your reality, not Silicon Valley's highlight reel.

**[CLOSING — 2:45–3:00]**

The Artificial Intelligence revolution isn't just about building smarter machines. It's about being smart enough to know where they work, and where they don't yet.

### 3. Design Direction

- **Font style:** Primary — Inter Bold (headings/stats), Secondary — Inter Regular (body text)
- **Primary color:** Dark Cyra #00838F — dominant brand color on buttons, key highlights, progress fills
- **Secondary colors:**
  - Viridian Green #009BA2 — secondary accents, badges
  - Tiffany Blue #0FABBC — info highlights, accent lines
  - Caribbean Green #00C5A2 — success states, positive moments
- **Text colors:**
  - Gunmetal #2C333A — primary text on light backgrounds
  - Dark Slate Gray #314A52 — headings
  - Rhythm #69779B — muted labels, subtext
  - White #FFFFFF — text on dark backgrounds
- **Background palette:**
  - White #FFFFFF — main/clean scenes
  - Magnolia #F4F3FE — soft alternate sections
  - Gunmetal #2C333A — dark scenes
  - Deep Green Cyan Turquoise #206D62 — footer/closing scenes
  - Police Blue #394A6D — dramatic/serious scenes
- **Accent palette (used sparingly):**
  - Old Rose #EA5F5E — error/failure stats
  - Corn #FBEB5B — warning/caution moments
  - Turkish Rose #BC658D — special emphasis tags
  - American Purple #432160 — premium section feel
- **Borders & dividers:** Chinese Silver #CCCCCC

- **B-roll type:** Abstract tech visuals, minimal flat icons, data visualization graphics, circuit/grid patterns — no stock photos of people
