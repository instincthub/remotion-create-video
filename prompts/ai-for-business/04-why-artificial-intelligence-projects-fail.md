## VIDEO SCRIPT

**Title:** Why Artificial Intelligence Projects Fail
**Slug:** why-artificial-intelligence-projects-fail
**Excerpt:** Artificial Intelligence is powerful, but building real-world applications is very different from traditional software. Here is what makes it risky, complex, and easy to get wrong.


What if I told you that your billion-naira Artificial Intelligence project could fail… because of dirty data?

Not bad code.
Not bad developers.
Just bad data.

Most people think Artificial Intelligence is just smarter software. It is not. It behaves very differently from traditional applications. And if you build it the same way, you will fail.

Let us start with the biggest difference.

First, data decides behaviour.

In traditional software, developers write code to a clear specification. The data is just input. If the logic is correct, the system behaves correctly.

But in Machine Learning systems, the data does not just test the system. It defines the system.

If your training data is biased, incomplete, messy, or manually corrected by humans, your model will learn those patterns. And here is the scary part. If you needed manual data cleaning during training, you will need it every single day in production.

Imagine training a model with a team of analysts manually fixing errors in spreadsheets. Then you launch the system expecting sub-second decisions. Who is fixing the data now?

If you cannot automate the cleaning, you cannot scale the solution.

Second, Artificial Intelligence makes mistakes.

Unlike traditional systems, which follow rules exactly, Machine Learning systems operate on probabilities. They make judgment calls.

And humans struggle with this.

When a human driver causes an accident, society shrugs and says, “humans make mistakes.” But when a self-driving system makes one error, headlines explode.

Artificial Intelligence systems are expected to make mistakes. In fact, they are designed to fail some test cases. If your model is perfect on training data, that is usually a red flag.

Which leads to the third difference.

Generalisation.

Humans are incredible at adapting. A driver trained in one country can adapt to a different country, different road signs, and even different vehicles.

That is generalisation.

Machine Learning systems try to do the same. But sometimes they do not generalise. They memorise.

This is called overfitting. Or, as some practitioners say, learning the deck.

When a system learns the deck, it performs brilliantly on known examples but collapses in new situations. And the real world is always new.

So engineers deliberately design models that are not perfect. They aim for balance. Good enough performance, but flexible enough to adapt.

That feels uncomfortable. But it is necessary.

Here is the big takeaway.

Artificial Intelligence projects fail not because the algorithms are weak. They fail because teams treat them like normal software projects.

If you are building an Artificial Intelligence system, ask yourself:

Is our data production ready?
Can we automate data cleaning?
Are we prepared for mistakes?
Are we testing for generalisation, not perfection?

Artificial Intelligence is powerful. But it is not magic.

It is data-driven, probabilistic, and imperfect by design.

Build for that reality, and you increase your chances of success.

Ignore it, and your next big project might become another expensive prototype that never goes live.
