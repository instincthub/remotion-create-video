## VIDEO SCRIPT

Imagine asking your phone a question and getting the exact answer in seconds.

When was Neil Armstrong born?

Instantly: August 5th, 1930.

It feels like magic. But the truth is simpler and more fascinating.

Today, most of the Artificial Intelligence we interact with is powered by massive web companies. Think Google, Amazon, Facebook, Apple. These companies dominate Artificial Intelligence because they dominate the web.

Here’s why that matters.

First, they have something incredibly powerful: data.
Millions, sometimes billions of users use their platforms every day. Every search, every click, every purchase generates data. And because their services are free, users rarely think twice about sharing it.

Artificial Intelligence feeds on data. The more you have, the better it performs.

Second, they solve simple problems at a massive scale.
Take question answering. When you ask, “When was Neil Armstrong born?” the system does not truly understand history like a human would. Instead, it scans enormous amounts of web content and finds patterns.

Across thousands of websites, you will see sentences like:

“Neil Armstrong was born on August 5th, 1930.”

When the same date appears again and again next to the words “Neil Armstrong” and “born,” a simple correlation algorithm can confidently extract the answer.

It works because the question is simple and the web is full of repeated information.

But here is the catch.

This approach works brilliantly when the data is redundant and public. It fails when data is rare, complex, or sensitive.

Imagine a police database containing only one sentence:

“John Edgar cannot be the brother of Steve Edgar because he was born two years before their parents first met.”

There is no repetition. No redundancy. No thousands of similar examples. A simple correlation algorithm would struggle badly.

That is the difference between web Artificial Intelligence and enterprise Artificial Intelligence.

Web companies also benefit from low risk.
If a search result is slightly off, it is inconvenient. If a movie recommendation is wrong, you scroll past it.

But in medicine, banking, insurance, or defence, mistakes are expensive. Sometimes life changing.

Web Artificial Intelligence systems usually:

Serve millions of users
Perform simple, narrow tasks
Have a low cost of errors
Make money indirectly through advertising or referrals
Rely on huge volumes of shared user data

Enterprise systems are the opposite:

Fewer users
More complex decisions
Higher cost of mistakes
Direct business impact
Often limited and sensitive data

So while web companies have built incredible Artificial Intelligence systems, copying their approach inside a business is not always straightforward.

The big lesson?

Artificial Intelligence success is not just about clever algorithms. It is about the type of problem, the availability of data, the tolerance for mistakes, and the business model behind it.

As we move beyond consumer apps into real business transformation, we must remember: what works on the web does not automatically work in your enterprise.

Artificial Intelligence is powerful. But context is everything.
