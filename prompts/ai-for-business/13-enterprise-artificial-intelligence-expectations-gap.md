Video Title: Why Enterprise Artificial Intelligence Projects Fail
Slug: enterprise-artificial-intelligence-expectations-gap
Excerpt: Artificial Intelligence in big companies is not like a web demo. Higher stakes. More complexity. Here is what most businesses underestimate and how to manage it properly.

## VIDEO SCRIPT

You want Artificial Intelligence in your company.

But here is the real question.

Are you building a demo.

Or an enterprise system that people must trust every single day?

We build airplanes with massive engineering teams.
Satellites.
Nuclear submarines.

They use advanced design tools.
Strict processes.
Component tracking.
Risk controls.

Now compare that to many Artificial Intelligence projects.

Small teams.
Spreadsheets.
Simple scripts.
Limited governance.

And yet we expect these systems to make high stakes decisions.

That is a mismatch of expectations.

Here is the problem.

Enterprise Artificial Intelligence is not the same as web company Artificial Intelligence.

A web platform can experiment.
If search results are slightly wrong, users tolerate it.

But if your bank’s Artificial Intelligence exposes the wrong financial data, that is not a small mistake.
That is a crisis.

So what makes enterprise Artificial Intelligence different?

First, business relevance.

Your Artificial Intelligence must make more money than it costs.
It must tie to measurable outcomes.
Shareholders expect that.
Executives demand it.

Second, stakeholder agreement.

Customers.
Regulators.
Internal teams.
They must trust the system.
If trust breaks, adoption dies.

Third, application complexity.

Artificial Intelligence is easy to try.
It is not easy to productionize.

Simple requirement.
Complex reality.
Data quality issues.
Integration problems.
Edge cases everywhere.

Fourth, correctness and consistency.

Is 90 percent accuracy enough?
Maybe for product recommendations.
Not for medical diagnosis.
Not for fraud detection.
Not for compliance decisions.

Fifth, decision consequence.

When the model fails, what happens?

Minor inconvenience.
Or financial damage.
Or legal exposure.

You must design for failure.
Because failure is guaranteed at some point.

Sixth, data strategy and governance.

Where does your data come from?
Is it stable?
Is it archived?
Can you retrain when drift happens?

Artificial Intelligence systems are not install and forget.
They are living systems.

Seventh, skills and resources.

Do you have engineers to maintain it?
Monitor it?
Audit it?
Improve it?

Launching is easy.
Owning is hard.

And finally, ethics and regulation.

Are you compliant?
Are you transparent?
Are you creating reputational risk?

Now visualize this.

On one side, a web Artificial Intelligence experiment.
High flexibility.
Low consequence.
Fast iteration.

On the other side, enterprise Artificial Intelligence.
High governance.
High consequence.
High complexity.

Different radar shapes.
Different risk profiles.

If you treat them the same, you fail.

So what should you do?

Adopt an engineering mindset.

Define measurable business value.
Map stakeholders.
Assess risk tolerance.
Plan for monitoring.
Budget for maintenance.
Design governance from day one.

Stop thinking like a feature builder.

Start thinking like a systems engineer.

Because Artificial Intelligence is not magic.

It is engineering.

And in the enterprise world, engineering discipline is what separates hype from impact.
