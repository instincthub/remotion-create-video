## VIDEO SCRIPT

**Episode 2: "The Jobs Your Child May Never Get, And the Ones That Will Pay a Fortune"**

---

Imagine spending four years getting a university degree. Taking on debt. Working internships. Finally landing that entry-level job.

Now imagine Artificial Intelligence doing that job better, faster, and for a fraction of the cost.

This is not a hypothetical. This is happening right now.

Harvard Business School research published in 2026 revealed something that should alarm every parent in this room. After the launch of ChatGPT, job postings for structured, repetitive work dropped by 13%. And here is the part that surprises most people. These were not factory jobs. These were white-collar jobs. Office jobs. The kind of jobs we told our children to work hard in school to get.

The old rules of career safety have changed. Completely.

So let us talk honestly about what is disappearing, and what is being born.

The jobs facing the steepest drop are the ones built almost entirely on tasks that Artificial Intelligence can now do faster and cheaper. Data entry clerks. Artificial Intelligence can automate roughly 95% of those tasks today. Telemarketers and call centre agents. Artificial Intelligence now handles over 70% of routine customer inquiries. Bookkeepers and basic accountants. The World Economic Forum lists them among the fastest-declining roles by 2030. Administrative assistants and legal assistants. Artificial Intelligence platforms are already performing contract reviews and legal research. Translators and interpreters. Microsoft Research ranks this as the number one occupation for Artificial Intelligence applicability. And entry-level software developers. Yes, even coding. Anthropic reports that 75% of basic programming tasks are now highly exposed to Artificial Intelligence automation.

But here is the part of the story nobody is telling loudly enough.

The entry-level crisis is the most urgent problem facing young people today.

Goldman Sachs data shows that unemployment among 20 to 30-year-olds in technology-exposed roles has risen by roughly 3 percentage points since early 2025. IDC's 2026 report found that 66% of enterprises are already reducing entry-level hiring as they deploy Artificial Intelligence. Gartner predicts that by the end of 2026, 20% of organisations will use Artificial Intelligence to eliminate more than half of their current middle management positions.

One former Fortune 500 chief executive described it this way. Companies are burning the bottom rungs of the career ladder to heat the house.

The ladder our children expected to climb is being dismantled while they are still in school.

So what do we do with this information?

We stop panicking and we start preparing. Because the same technology that is closing those doors is throwing open windows that lead to some of the most well-paid, most meaningful, most in-demand careers in human history.

Here is what the job market actually looks like for children who are prepared.

Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning Engineers earn an average of $206,000 per year. With projected job growth of 26% through 2033. Prompt Engineers, a job title that did not exist three years ago, already command salaries from $63,000 to over $300,000 at top Artificial Intelligence laboratories, with demand growing at 135% per year. Artificial Intelligence Ethics Officers and Responsible Artificial Intelligence Specialists earn between $90,000 and $180,000. Forrester predicts that 60% of Fortune 100 companies will appoint a dedicated head of Artificial Intelligence governance by late 2026. Artificial Intelligence Auditors and Compliance Managers earn $130,000 to $200,000 and above. Artificial Intelligence Safety and Alignment Researchers at leading laboratories are receiving total compensation packages exceeding $1 million. Data Scientists are projected to see 34% employment growth through 2034. The World Economic Forum lists Big Data Specialists as the single fastest-growing role on the entire planet.

And these are just the roles we can name today.

Almost 40% of chief executives report their companies entered entirely new industries because of Artificial Intelligence-driven opportunities. The Artificial Intelligence governance industry alone is projected to surpass $20 billion by 2026. New fields are forming right now around synthetic data, agentic Artificial Intelligence systems, and Artificial Intelligence-powered healthcare diagnostics.

Harvard Professor Karim Lakhani put it in a way every parent and teacher needs to hear.

Artificial Intelligence will not replace humans. But humans who use Artificial Intelligence will replace humans who do not.

The World Economic Forum projects that by 2030, 47% of work tasks will still be performed primarily by humans. Another 30% will be collaborative, humans and Artificial Intelligence working together. Only 22% will be handled mainly by technology alone.

The future of work is not a world without humans. It is a world where the humans who understand Artificial Intelligence will have an extraordinary advantage over those who do not.

And right now, the children who will hold that advantage are already being shaped. Not in classrooms, necessarily. But in homes where parents are paying attention.

In Episode 3, we will show you exactly what skills your child needs to be irreplaceable in this new world.

Because it is not just about coding. It is about something far more powerful.

**Design Direction**

- Font style: Montserrat ExtraBold or Inter Black for headlines and stats. Inter Regular for supporting labels and source credits.
- Motion style: A visual split between decline (red-tinted, downward movement, strikethroughs, fading) and growth (green-tinted, upward movement, counting up, brightening). This contrast is the visual spine of the episode.
- B-roll type: Abstract only. Downward particle flows for job decline sections. Upward-moving light streaks and expanding node networks for job growth sections. No real people or stock footage.
- Color transition rule: Scenes 1 to 5 lean dark and Old Rose. Scenes 6 to 8 transition to Caribbean Green and Dark Cyra. This mirrors the emotional arc of the script.
- Logo placement: Subtle bottom-right watermark throughout. Full-size reveal in Scene 8.
- Lower thirds: Source attributions (Harvard, Goldman Sachs, IDC, WEF) appear in Rhythm (#69779B) small text below each relevant stat.
